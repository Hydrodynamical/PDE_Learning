\begin{abstract}
Interactive scientific benchmarks aim to measure how language models choose
experiments, but recovery scores also depend on the observation model and
numerical implementation.
We audit a weak-form inverse-problem benchmark using 180 archived scored runs
from six models on 30 shared elliptic-equation instances, paired statistical
comparisons, and new numerical controls kept distinct from historical outcomes.
A fixed sine-query design recovers all 30 reconstructed tasks at the strict
error threshold using the protocol's minimum solve budget, so these tasks do
not establish a benefit from adaptive language-model measurement selection.
Free consistency checks supply additional observations, while absolute rounding
and small singular values make recovery sensitive to numerical representation.
An exact nonuniqueness result further shows why full floating-point matrix rank
cannot establish structural identifiability.
The audit provides reproducible diagnostics for separating observable agent
behavior from claims about experimental-design ability.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A scientific agent must choose observations that distinguish plausible
explanations. A benchmark can make this process inspectable by placing the agent
in a known inverse problem: the hidden parameters are available to the evaluator,
the measurement actions have mathematical definitions, and the tools expose the
information used by the agent. However, a high recovery score can also reflect
properties of the numerical environment. Before interpreting the score as
experimental-design ability, we must understand which parameters the observations
identify and how numerical error affects their recovery.

We investigate a benchmark in which a language model identifies the coefficients
of an elliptic differential equation by selecting weak-form test functions.
The solver handles quadrature and least squares; the agent chooses measurements,
inspects estimates, and decides when to stop. The setup is deliberately small
and instrumented. Unlike an open-ended discovery task, it permits direct analysis
of the joint measurement matrix and an exact specification of the correctness
event. ``Learn'' in the title means identification within an interaction, rather
than updating model weights or discovering an unknown equation family.

The benchmark first appeared in a Kaggle write-up \citep{kaggle2026pde}.
This paper reexamines its public six-model archive rather than treating the
original behavioral labels as established cognitive measurements.
We ask three questions: (i) how do the archived policies differ on shared tasks;
(ii) what recovery is possible with simple numerical query policies; and
(iii) which aspects of the protocol and discretization constrain the interpretation
of those comparisons?

Our contributions are an auditable formulation of the measurement task; a
reproducible analysis of 180 scored records with comparisons paired by problem
instance; and new controls that expose the distinction between structural
nonuniqueness, numerical conditioning, and measured agent behavior.
An exact polynomial-family result gives a concrete failure of unconditional
identifiability. Reconstructed measurements then quantify numerical and protocol
effects on the actual archived tasks. The fixed sine control succeeds on every reconstructed task, so the archived
results do not establish a benefit from adaptive language-model measurement
selection. The broader contribution is a case study of how omitted numerical
controls can change a scientific-agent benchmark's interpretation.

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Interactive scientific agents.}
DiscoveryWorld evaluates a broad scientific-discovery loop in simulated worlds
\citep{jansen2024discoveryworld}. BoxingGym studies automated experimental design
and model discovery across generative environments
\citep{gandhi2025boxinggym}. SciGym provides a systems-biology dry lab for
scientific-agent evaluation \citep{duan2025scigym}. Our task shares their
sequential observation--feedback structure. Its narrower contribution is a
finite-dimensional weak-form inverse problem in which the measurement matrix,
coefficient truth, and numerical sensitivities can be audited directly. We do
not claim to introduce interactive scientific evaluation itself.

\paragraph{Equation discovery and inverse problems.}
LLM-SR uses language models for scientific equation discovery, and LLM-SRBench
evaluates discovery from observations \citep{shojaee2025llmsr,shojaee2025llmsrbench}.
Weak SINDy uses integral formulations for PDE identification
\citep{messenger2021wsindy}. Here the operator family and polynomial basis are
known, and the agent chooses test functions while a supplied solver estimates
coefficients. This is a measurement-selection problem within a specified model,
rather than symbolic structure discovery. Optimal experimental design provides
a relevant reference \citep{mackay1992information,alexanderian2021oed}, but its
criteria require an explicit noise model, admissible designs, and appropriate
rank assumptions. We do not equate expression-family diversity with information
gain.

\paragraph{Identifiability and confidence.}
Identifiability depends on the observation operator. Results for boundary maps,
reaction--advection--diffusion processes, and parabolic observations
\citep{egger2014simultaneous,browning2024structural,browning2025parabolic}
do not by themselves guarantee uniqueness from one stationary solution and
source. Our analysis distinguishes this structural issue from finite-grid
conditioning. The confidence analysis uses an explicit coefficient-error event,
following the distinction between stated confidence and empirical accuracy
\citep{guo2017calibration}. The cognitive framework of \citet{burnell2026cognitive} contextualizes the
original behavioral labels; our diagnostics describe behavior under a specific
prompted protocol.

\section{Benchmark and Observation Model}
\label{sec:task}

\subsection{Hidden Equation and Linear Recovery}

The hidden boundary value problem is
\begin{equation}
 \begin{gathered}-(au')'+bu'+cu=f,\quad x\in[0,1],\\ u(0)=u(1)=0.\end{gathered}
 \label{eq:pde}
\end{equation}
Each field \(q\in\{a,b,c,f\}\) has the form
\(q(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\theta_{q,j}P_j(x)\), using ordinary shifted Legendre
polynomials on \([0,1]\). The polynomials are orthogonal but not orthonormal.
The generator samples coefficients and computes \(u\) numerically.
For an admissible test function \(\varphi_k\) vanishing at the endpoints,
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}&\int au'\varphi_k'\dd x+\int bu'\varphi_k\dd x+\int cu\varphi_k\dd x\\ &\qquad =r_k,\qquad r_k=\int f\varphi_k\dd x.\end{aligned}
 \label{eq:weak}
\end{equation}
All integrals are over \([0,1]\). Define
\begin{align}
 D_{kj}&=\int P_j u'\varphi_k'\dd x,& B_{kj}&=\int P_j u'\varphi_k\dd x,\nonumber\\
 C_{kj}&=\int P_j u\varphi_k\dd x,& F_{kj}&=\int P_j\varphi_k\dd x.
 \label{eq:matrices}
\end{align}
The tool separately minimizes \(\|A\theta-r\|_2\) for the operator, where
\(A=[D\mid B\mid C]\in\R^{K\times3N}\), and \(\|F\theta_f-r\|_2\) for
the source. Although the prompt describes \(4N\) unknown coefficients, the
returned source moments already provide the right-hand side of the operator
system. The requirement of \(K\ge4N\) before solving is a protocol rule,
rather than a rank guarantee.

The reference implementation uses second-order finite differences for the
solution, a fourth-order interior derivative with boundary stencils, and
composite Simpson quadrature on the solution grid. It rounds each stored matrix
entry and right-hand-side value to 12 decimal places. The solve uses unweighted
least squares. The model sees coefficients rounded to eight decimal places;
final scoring uses the internally retained unrounded estimate. Symbolic test
functions are differentiated by a fourth-order centered stencil with step
\(10^{-6}\), independently of the solution-grid spacing.
These choices matter when singular values are small. Table~\ref{tab:difficulty}
also shows that the three difficulty labels are bundled settings; differences
cannot be attributed solely to dimension.

\subsection{Error and Identifiability}
\label{sec:identifiability}

For each field, the scoring event uses maximum absolute coefficient deviation:
\begin{equation}
 E_q=\max_j|\widehat\theta_{q,j}-\theta_{q,j}|,\qquad
 E=\sum_{q\in\{a,b,c,f\}}E_q.
 \label{eq:error}
\end{equation}
Our primary threshold is \(E<0.01\); \(E<0.1\) is a sensitivity analysis.
These are not function-error thresholds on held-out points. The separately
reported function error is bounded by \(NE\), since \(|P_j(x)|\le1\), but
equal numerical thresholds are not equivalent.

Full column rank of the joint matrix \(A\), rather than each coefficient block,
is necessary and sufficient for unique unconstrained least-squares operator
coefficients. Small singular values can amplify discretization and rounding
errors. Floating-point rank is a property of the reconstructed finite matrix;
it is not a uniqueness theorem for the underlying observation operator.

\begin{proposition}[An admissible nonidentifiable family]
\label{prop:nullity}
Let \(u(x)=x(1-x)\), \(N\ge2\), and
\(T(\delta a,\delta b,\delta c)=-(\delta a u')'+\delta b u'+\delta c u\)
with domain \(\Poly_{N-1}^3\). Then \(T\) has rank \(N+2\) and nullity
\(2N-2\). Every element of its kernel leaves all continuum weak-form
measurements unchanged at fixed \(u,f\).
\end{proposition}

A proof and explicit elliptic examples appear in Appendix~\ref{app:proof}.
This is a counterexample within the permitted class, rather than a claim that
the 30 sampled instances belong to this quadratic-solution family. It rules out
unconditional identifiability. Separate audits are needed to characterize
recoverability on the sampled tasks.

\section{Evaluation Design}
\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Archive and Provenance}

The public Kaggle dataset, \emph{Elliptic Differential Equation LLM Reasoning},
version 1, contains 180 scored logs: six models, three difficulty settings,
and seeds 1--10 in every cell. The same 30 coefficient truths are shared across
models. The archive was released on 16 April 2026; the provider model IDs are
listed in Appendix~\ref{app:repro}.
All logs record the \texttt{standard} prompt, called enhanced in the write-up.
The archive does not contain a baseline-prompt ablation, repeated stochastic
draws per model--task pair, or a complete record of earlier failed attempts and
retries. Rates are consequently conditional on inclusion in the scored archive.

We verify the archive checksum, cell membership, shared truths, finite scores,
and agreement with the saved sweep summary before analysis. The exact source
revision and temperature are not recorded per run. A public implementation
snapshot postdates some runs. Our new experiments use the current pinned
numerical environment; they do not recreate historical provider calls.

\subsection{Numerical Controls and Replay}
\label{sec:controls}

For the controls, we regenerate all 30 tasks from their recorded seeds, verify
exact equality with saved coefficient truths, and evaluate
non-language-model query policies under the same accepted-query limits and
supplied query/solve interface. The policies use no hidden coefficients, solution values, measurement responses,
or diagnostics to select expressions. Fixed sine chooses
\(\varphi_k=\sin(k\pi x)\), \(k=1,\ldots,B\), where \(B=6N\) is the
stored-query budget. Boundary Legendre chooses \(x(1-x)P_{k-1}(x)\), using
factored expressions for numerical stability. Random mixed draws uniformly
from five admissible families, using ten independently seeded query lists per
setting, reused across the ten PDE seeds. Its fixed parameter ranges and exact
expressions are released in the experiment output. All controls solve at every
query count from \(4N\) through \(6N\); additional solves do not alter their
predefined query lists. These controls test whether simple nonadaptive policies suffice on these
instances; they do not reproduce the models' free-action
or stopping decisions.

For the numerical audit, we replay the archived query expressions, compare the
returned source moments with the logs, and reconstruct joint matrix spectra.
We report both raw and unit-column-norm spectra, with explicit relative singular
value cutoffs rather than a binary rank label alone. We additionally multiply
each reconstructed row and its source moment by a common amplitude before
12-decimal rounding, leaving the query sets fixed and refitting all stored rows. This audit scores
8-decimal diagnostic estimates, unlike unrounded primary final scores. In exact arithmetic this common scaling leaves least squares unchanged;
its effect here tests the absolute quantization rule. It is a post hoc numerical
sensitivity analysis, rather than an optimized query policy or a model rerun.

\subsection{Statistical Unit and Behavioral Diagnostics}

We also refine the forward and quadrature grids together by factors two and
four. Original-resolution primary controls use the native dense solver.
Refinements use an independently assembled banded solver for the same finite-
difference equations; we validate its solution and score sensitivity against
native solves on all 30 tasks. Refinement changes the numerical environment,
rather than adding independent tasks or measuring observation-noise robustness.

The comparison unit is the shared PDE seed within each difficulty, not an
individual coefficient, solve, or confidence report. Each model has ten tasks
per setting. We report Wilson 95\% intervals for marginal success proportions
and paired task resampling for success differences (20,000 draws; seed 20261020). Exploratory pairwise tests
condition on discordant tasks, with Holm correction across the 15 model pairs
within each difficulty. There are no repeated model draws from which to estimate
within-task response variance. Empirical paired-bootstrap intervals can
degenerate when all ten observed task differences agree. These intervals describe uncertainty under task
resampling assumptions and do not resolve selection into the scored archive.

For confidence, a report is paired only with a solve returned on a strictly
earlier transcript turn. The event is \(E_q<0.1\), separately for
\(q\in\{a,b,c\}\). All reports and coefficient blocks within a run are
kept together when resampling; run-balanced scores avoid giving a model more
weight merely because it reports more often. Action counts, first-to-last solve
improvement, and expression-family entropy are descriptive diagnostics. They do
not independently validate planning, learning, or information gain.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Archived Recovery Differs, with Limited Resolution}

Figure~\ref{fig:success} shows the archived success counts. At \(E<0.01\),
Gemini 3.1 Pro succeeds on \(10/10\), \(9/10\), and \(10/10\) easy, medium,
and hard tasks; Claude Opus 4.6 achieves \(10/10\), \(9/10\), and \(8/10\).
GPT-5.4 achieves \(7/10\), \(3/10\), and \(1/10\). The other models also
vary substantially across settings and seeds. The regenerated figure corrects
the write-up asset's hard GPT-5.4 mini count from \(0/10\) to \(2/10\),
which agrees with both individual logs and the archived sweep summary.

With ten tasks, even \(10/10\) has a Wilson 95\% interval of approximately
\([0.72,1.00]\), and \(9/10\) has \([0.60,0.98]\). Thus the heatmap is
not a precise model ranking. All pairwise task-level comparisons and confidence
intervals are released with the analysis; differences in this selected archive
should not be generalized to end-to-end provider reliability.
Only one of 45 exploratory pairwise comparisons has adjusted \(p<0.05\)
under the stated within-difficulty Holm procedure: Gemini 3.1 Pro versus
Claude Sonnet 4 on hard tasks (\(10/10\) versus \(0/10\), adjusted
\(p=0.0293\)). There is no correction across the three difficulty families;
this finding does not establish a global model ordering.

\subsection{Simple Nonadaptive Queries Suffice on These Tasks}
\label{sec:baseline_results}

The fixed sine policy reaches \(E<0.01\) on all ten tasks in each difficulty,
already at the minimum \(4N\) stored queries (8, 12, and 16). It retains
30/30 success at the full \(6N\) budget. Boundary Legendre succeeds on 29/30
at full budget. Random mixed succeeds on 100/100 easy, 92/100 medium, and
85/100 hard policy--task combinations (Table~\ref{tab:controls}); these reuse
ten PDE instances per setting and are not 100 independent tasks.
Across random query-list replicates, hard success ranges from 6/10 to 10/10.
Figure~\ref{fig:controls} shows recovery at every permitted solve count.

These controls show that the archived tasks can be solved by a simple
nonadaptive design with the supplied numerical tools. They do not show a
statistically established advantage over the best archived model, nor isolate
reasoning from parsing and stopping behavior. Because exact historical internal
matrices were not retained, we compare newly reconstructed controls with
archived outcomes transparently rather than claiming a prospective provider
comparison. The evidence does not support a claim that language-model adaptation
is necessary for recovery on this task set.

Grid refinement improves numerical errors: for fixed sine at full budget,
median \(E\) changes from \(9.47\times10^{-5}\) to
\(2.18\times10^{-6}\) on easy, \(1.32\times10^{-3}\) to
\(2.82\times10^{-5}\) on medium, and \(5.65\times10^{-4}\) to
\(1.56\times10^{-5}\) on hard when the grid is refined fourfold.
At twofold refinement, random mixed reaches 100/100 hard successes and
boundary Legendre reaches 10/10 medium successes. The original-grid native and
banded solves agree on both success thresholds at every checkpoint across all
360 paired policy--task runs. Thus the banded implementation does not change
these threshold comparisons, although individual numerical errors differ.

\subsection{What Numerical Reconstruction Can Establish}
\label{sec:numerics}

All 30 coefficient truths regenerate exactly, and the reconstructed reference
implementation reproduces all 2,874 archived query source moments exactly at
the stored precision. Source-moment agreement does not test the historical forward solution or
unretained operator matrix: the source moment depends only on \(f\), the test
function, and quadrature. It does not establish exact historical solve replay: across 687 solves, the median maximum deviation from the logged
8-decimal coefficient vectors is \(2.55\times10^{-5}\), and the largest is
3.50. The archive lacks historical internal matrices and sufficient environment
provenance to localize every discrepancy. New controls and spectral diagnostics
must therefore be identified as reconstructions.

All 180 reconstructed final stored-query matrices have full column rank at
NumPy's default floating-point tolerance. After unit-column scaling, however,
the median \(\sigma_{\min}/\sigma_{\max}\) is
\(2.26\times10^{-7}\), \(5.53\times10^{-10}\), and
\(1.80\times10^{-10}\) for easy, medium, and hard runs. At a declared
relative cutoff of \(10^{-8}\), 13/60, 42/60, and 37/60 matrices are rank
deficient. These are diagnostics of limited numerical resolution, not continuum
nonuniqueness certificates for the sampled instances.

Common-amplitude scaling changes the reconstruction's \(E<0.01\) counts
(Figure~\ref{fig:audit}). At amplitudes \(10^{-3},1,10^3\), the counts
are respectively 24/50/53 of 60 easy runs, 11/36/48 medium runs, and 19/28/33
hard runs. No additional test functions are selected: the exact-arithmetic measurements
differ only by a common nonzero factor, while their relative quantization
precision changes.
The changed counts show that absolute rounding and ill conditioning can alter
a success event even when the underlying span of measurements is unchanged.
They do not establish that larger amplitude universally improves recovery.

\subsection{Information Use and Confidence}

The archive contains 459 free consistency checks; 457 check expressions are
absent as literal strings from earlier stored-query histories. Literal novelty
does not prove semantic independence, but each check exposes an actual source
moment. Thus stored-query counts alone do not equate the information acquired
by two policies. This behavior is permitted by the protocol.

The confidence analysis finds 733 reports, of which 707 follow a solve and
26 are excluded. The retained reports yield 2,121 coefficient observations.
Causal matching changes the solve assigned to 71 reports relative to the legacy
query-count rule; 827 observations lie on interior confidence-bin boundaries
that the older plots could count twice. Corrected calibration curves and the
Kaggle action, improvement, and diversity figures appear in
Appendix~\ref{app:behavior}. Their interpretation remains descriptive: repeated
reports are dependent, prompt instructions affect action use, and expression
families are not independent measurement directions.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}

The audit reveals three requirements for interpreting measurement-selection
benchmarks. First, the observation model must distinguish identifiable
parameters from equivalent operators. A finite matrix's numerical rank does not
replace this analysis. Second, normalization, precision, and discretization must
be specified and tested. Changing a mathematical representation without adding
information should not silently change the claimed ability being measured.
Third, every truth-dependent observation must enter the information accounting,
including diagnostics exposed as free tools.

Our evidence is deliberately bounded. The archive contains 30 tasks and one
scored response trajectory per model--task pair, rather than a prospective
all-attempt study. Difficulty settings conflate dimension, scale, sparsity, and
resolution. This spatially one-dimensional equation is mathematically an ODE
boundary-value problem; the task does not test multidimensional PDE structure. We cannot reconstruct missing attempt histories or exact provider
settings. The structural proposition concerns an admissible subclass and does
not determine the continuum rank of every sampled operator. Reconstructed
spectra, amplitude tests, and numerical controls inherit the current solver and
must not be presented as historical model reruns.

A prospective benchmark should preregister the task distribution, retain every
attempt and failure, fix model versions and generation settings, and compare
fixed, randomized, and adaptive designs under a common observation budget.
It should normalize admissible tests, specify a noise or precision model,
characterize joint observability, and include prediction under independent
sources or boundary data where coefficient uniqueness is required. Confidence
scores should use a defined event and a declared reporting schedule. These are
requirements for a stronger experimental claim, rather than quantities that
can be inferred from the present archive.

\section{Conclusion}

Weak-form inverse problems make parts of scientific-agent evaluation directly
inspectable. The archived models differ in recovery and interaction behavior,
but an interpretation in terms of experimental-design ability requires controls
for the observation model, numerical conditioning, and information accounting.
A fixed sine design suffices on every reconstructed task, making numerical
baselines essential to the interpretation of model scores. The released
analysis provides checks for this benchmark and a concrete
basis for designing a more controlled prospective study.

\section*{Impact Statement}

This work studies a small mathematical environment for evaluating scientific
agents. Its immediate use is methodological: making evaluation claims traceable
to observations, numerical assumptions, and complete records. Overinterpreting
such scores could distort comparisons of scientific capabilities; the audit
limits that risk by documenting what the measurements support. We do not infer
readiness for autonomous scientific deployment from these experiments.

\section{Structural Nonuniqueness: Proof and Examples}
\label{app:proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:nullity}]
Let \(\Poly_d\) denote polynomials of degree at most \(d\).
For \(u=x(1-x)\), all three terms in \(T\) lie in \(\Poly_{N+1}\),
so the rank is at most \(N+2\). The reaction component contains
\(u\Poly_{N-1}\). Taking constant \(\delta a\) gives a nonzero constant
because \(-u''=2\), and taking constant \(\delta b\) gives \(u'=1-2x\).
These span \(\Poly_1\). Polynomial division by the quadratic \(u\) gives
\(\Poly_{N+1}=u\Poly_{N-1}\oplus\Poly_1\), proving surjectivity and rank
\(N+2\). The domain has dimension \(3N\), so rank--nullity gives
\(\dim\ker T=2N-2\). Multiplication by any admissible test function and
integration by parts shows that a kernel element changes no weak measurement.
\end{proof}

For \(N=2\), \(u=x(1-x)\) and \(f=3\), the operators
\[
 a=\tfrac32+\varepsilon,\qquad b=\varepsilon(4x-2),\qquad c=-8\varepsilon
\]
all satisfy the same equation, since
\(2\varepsilon+\varepsilon(4x-2)(1-2x)-8\varepsilon x(1-x)=0\).
For example \(\varepsilon=-1/8\) preserves positive diffusion and gives
nonnegative reaction. For \(N\ge3\), the base choice
\(a=1,b=0,c=1,f=2+u\) belongs to the class. Sufficiently small perturbations
in every kernel direction preserve positive diffusion and reaction, giving a relatively open \((2N-2)\)-dimensional family in the affine kernel
of admissible coefficient tuples with identical observations.
Neither construction is asserted to occur among the 30 sampled tasks.
The audit script also verifies ranks over exact rational arithmetic for
\(N=2,\ldots,8\); these checks supplement the proof rather than replace it.

\section{Data, Implementation, and Statistical Details}
\label{app:repro}

The primary input is the public Kaggle dataset \emph{Elliptic Differential
Equation LLM Reasoning}, version 1. The repository's
\path{paper_artifacts/data_manifest.json} records the dataset URL, version,

\section{Behavioral Figures and Their Interpretation}
\label{app:behavior}

The figures below regenerate the relevant Kaggle diagnostics from the full
archive. They retain the original operational questions while correcting data
or plotting discrepancies. For action counts, stacked marginal medians need not
sum to the median total action count. For improvement,
\(R=E_{\mathrm{first}}/E_{\mathrm{last}}\); missing ratios, including
single-solve runs, receive the historical display default \(R=1\). Large ratios
can reflect poor initial estimates or the ordinary benefit of additional data.

Query-family entropy is
\(H=-\sum_{i=1}^5p_i\log_2p_i/\log_2 5\), with \(0\log0=0\).
The five categories are polynomial, trigonometric, exponential, localized, and
other, assigned by a heuristic string classifier. The archived taxonomy is
recorded at the final solve; later queries in six runs are absent from it.
A high entropy does not imply linearly independent or useful measurements.

The confidence plots use disjoint bins \([l,h)\), with 1 included in the last
bin, and show mean confidence against empirical correctness. Reports are
matched only to strictly earlier solves, since a solve requested in a report's
own response has not yet been returned. The corrected 707 reports contribute
2,121 dependent observations for \(a,b,c\), excluding \(f\). Individual
coefficient reports are not treated as independent tasks.

For run \(i\) with \(m_i\) retained coefficient observations, the run-balanced
Brier score is
\[
 \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n\frac{1}{m_i}\sum_{r=1}^{m_i}(p_{ir}-y_{ir})^2.
\]
We also report the signed gap obtained by replacing the square with
\(p_{ir}-y_{ir}\); positive values indicate average overconfidence.
This gap is calibration in the large, not expected calibration error or a full
calibration criterion. Intervals resample whole supported runs with 20,000 draws.
There are 179 supported runs: Gemini Flash hard has nine and all other cells ten.
Run balancing does not align reporting stages, query counts, or base accuracies.
Thus these scores cannot compare intrinsic metacognitive ability without a
matched reporting schedule and correctness distribution.

\section{Why Legacy Sensitivity Scores Are Not Uncertainty Estimates}

The archived code forms a truncated pseudoinverse \(H_k\) of
\(A_k^\top A_k\), discarding singular values below \(10^{-10}\) times the
largest. It summarizes each coefficient block using
\(s_k(q)=\sqrt{\sum_{i\in q}(H_k)_{ii}/N}\).
A truncated inverse can assign zero weight to unresolved directions. Without
an observation-noise model and rank conditions, it is not a calibrated
coefficient covariance. Separate block values also cannot diagnose dependencies
between blocks.

The monitoring routine ranks values using double \texttt{argsort}, without
averaging ties; it is not standard tie-aware Spearman correlation. The control
routine applies a Sherman--Morrison-style update to a truncated pseudoinverse,
which need not agree with a freshly recomputed truncated inverse, and compares
reductions across blocks for the chosen next row rather than across candidate
queries. We retain these source fields for provenance but do not use them as
evidence of calibrated uncertainty or optimal experimental design.
