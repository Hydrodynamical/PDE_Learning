\begin{abstract}
We study how language models choose and use measurements when identifying an
unknown differential equation. In this benchmark, a model selects weak-form test
functions under a query budget and uses numerical tools to fit and check the
hidden coefficients. The interaction records measurement choices, verification,
error reduction, and confidence alongside final accuracy. We analyze 180 scored
runs from six models on 30 shared tasks. On medium tasks, Gemini 3.1 Pro and
Claude Opus 4.6 both achieve 9/10 success, but their mean consistency-check
counts are 2.3 and 7.6. Equal aggregate success thus coexists with different
patterns of tool use. Numerical controls provide a reference: a fixed sine
design solves all 30 reconstructed tasks and succeeds on 92\%, 91\%, and 98\%
of 100 fresh tasks in each of three settings at the minimum query budget.
The resulting records support a study of experimental behavior, while the
benefit of adaptive measurement selection remains an open question. We release
the environment, analysis, and numerical controls.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Scientific investigation requires choosing what to measure, interpreting the
result, and deciding what to do next. We study these decisions in a small
mathematical environment: a language model probes an unknown elliptic equation,
uses numerical tools to estimate its coefficients, and checks or revises the
estimate. The model selects the experimental sequence; the tools perform the
linear algebra.

An answer-only score leaves much of this process unobserved. A model may recover
the coefficients on its first solve, substantially improve an initially poor
estimate, or repeatedly check an inaccurate answer. Our benchmark records the
measurements and tool interactions alongside a mathematically defined error.
It was introduced in the Metacognition track of Kaggle's Measuring Progress
Toward AGI competition \citep{kaggle2026pde,burnell2026cognitive}. The central
question is how models with different or similar outcomes use the same
experimental interface.

We make three contributions: a tool-based environment connecting measurement
choices to known coefficient truth; an analysis of outcome and behavioral
profiles for six models on 30 shared tasks; and numerical controls that establish
a reference for interpreting those profiles. In the archived runs, Gemini 3.1
Pro and Claude Opus 4.6 have equal medium-task success counts but differ in
verification frequency and expression-family diversity. Examining tool use,
error change, and reported confidence makes these distinctions visible without
combining them into a single cognitive score.

The numerical reference is also informative. A fixed sine query sequence solves
all 30 reconstructed archived tasks, showing that adaptive measurement selection
is not necessary for recovery on this set. The model experiments therefore
study how agents navigate a controlled investigation. The controls and
identifiability analysis clarify how coefficient recovery depends on the inverse
problem and its numerical implementation.

\section{Task and Benchmark Construction}
\label{sec:task}

\subsection{An Unknown Equation and a Measurement Budget}

The hidden equation is
\begin{equation}
\begin{gathered}
 -(a(x)u'(x))'+b(x)u'(x)+c(x)u(x)=f(x),\\
 x\in(0,1),\qquad u(0)=u(1)=0.
\end{gathered}
\label{eq:pde}
\end{equation}
The diffusion, transport, reaction, and source fields \(a,b,c,f\) are
represented as \(q(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\theta_{q,j}P_j(x)\), where \(P_j\)
are shifted Legendre polynomials and the coefficients are sampled. A seed fixes
the coefficient vectors and the numerically computed response \(u\). The model can inspect \(u\) and its
derivative but must recover the hidden fields. The reference prompt supplies the
operator family and parameter count, while the numerical solver handles the
basis representation. ``Learning'' means parameter recovery within a session. In one spatial
dimension the task is an ODE boundary-value problem representing an elliptic
PDE setting, not a test of multidimensional PDE structure.

An experiment consists of choosing a test function \(\varphi\) that vanishes at
both endpoints. Integration by parts gives
\begin{equation}
 \int_0^1 au'\varphi'\,\dd x + \int_0^1 bu'\varphi\,\dd x
 + \int_0^1 cu\varphi\,\dd x = \int_0^1 f\varphi\,\dd x.
 \label{eq:weak}
\end{equation}
The query returns the source moment on the right. Internally, the tool uses
\(u\) and the coefficient basis to assemble a row of the linear inverse
problem. Accumulated measurements support least-squares estimates of the
operator coefficients and a separate fit of the source. For example,
\(\varphi(x)=\sin(\pi x)\) probes one source moment; changing its frequency or
shape changes the constraint imposed on the unknown equation.

This construction gives experimental choices a concrete mathematical role.
Test functions that look different need not provide independent constraints,
and an ill-conditioned set of measurements can fit well while recovering
coefficients poorly. The question for the agent is what to do with the feedback
it receives. The environment permits choosing more measurements, inspecting
term contributions, resolving the system, and checking a prediction.

The budget counts stored solver rows. A free consistency check also reveals a

behavioral profile rather than treating the stored-query count as the entire
cost of an investigation.

\subsection{A Ground-Truth Outcome}

For field \(q\), let \(\theta_q\) be its true coefficient vector. The score is
\begin{equation}
 E_q=\max_j|\widehat\theta_{q,j}-\theta_{q,j}|,\qquad
 E=\sum_{q\in\{a,b,c,f\}}E_q.
 \label{eq:error}
\end{equation}
We use \(E<0.01\) as the primary success event and \(E<0.1\) as a threshold
sensitivity check. This is error in the prescribed coefficient coordinates;
it is distinct from a weak residual or a held-out-grid function error. The
continuous errors remain available to the evaluator for studying changes within
a run. Ground-truth coefficient errors are not tool feedback shown to the model.

\section{Evaluation Protocol and Behavioral Measures}
\label{sec:methods}
\label{sec:profile}

\subsection{Shared Tasks and Archived Runs}

The version-1 Kaggle archive contains 180 scored runs from six models on three
settings and ten seeds per setting. A task is one generated coefficient tuple
and its numerical solution; a run is one model's interaction with that task.
All six models share the same 30 tasks. Each record contains the transcript,
parsed actions, measurements, solve outputs, errors, and available confidence
reports. We verify cell sizes, shared truths, finite scores, and agreement with
the saved sweep summary. The run-level figures use locally retained records
whose regenerated summaries match the saved archive analysis; file provenance
and validation details are given in Appendix~\ref{app:repro}.

All runs record the \texttt{standard} condition, called \emph{enhanced} in the
Kaggle write-up. The archive has no baseline-prompt ablation, repeated model draws
per task, or complete history of failed attempts and retries. Exact provider
IDs and recorded settings are in Appendix~\ref{app:repro}. The available
reference implementation describes the intended assistance and interface, but
the archive does not pin the full source and system prompt used for every run.
Consequently, the analyses describe the retained scored interactions rather
than end-to-end provider reliability.

Comparisons use the shared task as the statistical unit. We report Wilson 95\%
intervals for success proportions and paired task-level comparisons. With ten
tasks per model and setting, these comparisons have limited resolution.
Numerical controls use reconstructed tasks and a fixed sine policy on 300 fresh
tasks; they make no new model calls and are kept separate from archived outcomes.

\subsection{Behavioral Measures}

We examine four observable aspects of the session, motivated by questions about
planning, within-session improvement, diversity, and monitoring. The metrics
retain distinct units and interpretations.

\paragraph{Tool use.}
Counts of solves, consistency checks, and term-integral calls describe how a
model allocates interaction to fitting, verification, and inspecting the equation.
We summarize each action type separately. Counts measure activity under a prompt
that encourages iteration; they do not by themselves establish the quality of a
plan.

\paragraph{Error change.}
For runs with at least two recorded solves,
\(R=E_{\mathrm{first}}/E_{\mathrm{last}}\) describes the net error reduction.
All 159 repeated-solve runs have valid ratios; the 21 single-solve runs are
reported separately. A large ratio can reflect a poor starting estimate, so
we interpret improvement together with the endpoint. The metric measures
within-session error change; strategic adaptation requires further evidence
from the intervening actions.

\paragraph{Expression-family diversity.}
Normalized entropy \(H=-\sum_i p_i\log_2(p_i)/\log_2 5\) summarizes the
frequencies of five expression categories: polynomial, trigonometric,
exponential, localized, and other. This is a heuristic classification of
expression forms. Independence and usefulness are properties of the resulting
measurement matrix, so entropy is not an information-gain measure.

\paragraph{Confidence and correctness.}
The reference prompt asks for a probability that each coefficient vector is
``within 0.1 of the true value,'' without specifying a norm. We operationalize
that event as maximum absolute coordinate error \(E_q<0.1\), for
\(q=a,b,c\). We focus on the operator coefficients: the separately fitted
source \(f\) satisfies \(E_f<0.1\) at all 687 recorded solves, so including
it would add an always-correct outcome class. Each report is matched to the
most recent solve returned on a strictly earlier turn. Reliability curves pool
reports within confidence bins. Run-balanced Brier scores and signed
confidence-minus-correctness gaps provide complementary summaries; negative gaps mean
average underconfidence for this event. Uncertainty estimates resample whole
runs, retaining all reports and coefficient blocks within each run. Reporting
stages vary across models, so these summaries characterize confidence under the
observed protocol rather than a matched test of intrinsic metacognitive ability.

\section{Model Outcomes and Experimental Behavior}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Coefficient Recovery}

Figure~\ref{fig:success} compares success across the six models and three
settings. At \(E<0.01\), Gemini 3.1 Pro succeeds on 10/10, 9/10, and 10/10
easy, medium, and hard tasks; Claude Opus 4.6 achieves 10/10, 9/10, and 8/10.
GPT-5.4 drops sharply: 7/10, 3/10, and 1/10. Success is not
monotone across settings for every model, and the difficulty labels change
several generator parameters together. With ten tasks per cell, even 10/10
has a Wilson 95\% interval of approximately [0.72, 1.00]. Appendix~\ref{app:repro}
gives these intervals and paired comparisons; the released numerical summaries
also report the relaxed threshold \(E<0.1\).

\subsection{Tool Use}

Models with similar success rates use the interface differently
(Figure~\ref{fig:actions}). Gemini 3.1 Pro and Claude Opus 4.6 both achieve
9/10 medium successes: eight tasks are solved by both, with one success unique
to each model. Yet Pro makes an average of 2.3 consistency checks and 3.1 solves,
compared with 7.6 checks and 4.0 solves for Opus.

More activity also coexists with poor recovery. On hard tasks, GPT-5.4 makes
an average of 20.6 term-integral calls and 6.5 solves, compared with 0.9 and
3.0 for Gemini 3.1 Pro, while their success counts are 1/10 and 10/10.
These counts describe how models allocate their interaction budget; they do
not establish a causal effect of checking or diagnostic activity.

\subsection{Within-Session Improvement}

The improvement profiles vary substantially across models
(Figure~\ref{fig:improvement}). Claude Sonnet 4 reduces error by a median factor
of approximately 193 on medium tasks, yet succeeds on only 6/10. Its four
failed runs all improve, by factors of 3.1--24.4, while still finishing above
the success threshold. Error reduction and final accuracy answer different
questions.

Means and medians also tell different parts of the story. Gemini 3.1 Pro has
10/10 hard successes and a median improvement ratio of 1.0, but a mean of
approximately 833, driven by a few large reductions. The individual points
show this variation directly. Ratios compare recorded solves; the submitted
errors agree with last-solve errors to within \(1.34\times10^{-8}\), with no
change in success classification. Appendix~\ref{app:behavior} gives the
sample and metric definitions.

\subsection{Query Diversity}

Successful models can use quite different mixtures of test functions
(Figure~\ref{fig:diversity}). On medium tasks, Gemini 3.1 Pro and Claude Opus
4.6 have median family entropies of 0.38 and 0.67, despite equal success counts.
On hard tasks, Pro's median entropy falls to 0.24 while it solves all ten tasks.
A narrow family can therefore be effective. Entropy describes the form of the
chosen expressions; the rank and conditioning of the resulting measurements
determine what they reveal about the equation.

\section{Numerical Controls and Interpretation}
\label{sec:validation}

The task's mathematical structure gives a way to investigate the behavioral
patterns. A fixed sine policy, \(\varphi_k(x)=\sin(k\pi x)\), selects
measurements without feedback. On the 30 reconstructed archived tasks it
achieves 30/30 success at the first permitted solve, using 8, 12, or 16 queries
depending on the setting. Boundary-Legendre tests achieve 29/30 at full budget.
Thus a single well-chosen family can be effective; broad expression diversity
and repeated revision are not prerequisites for solving these instances.

We also evaluate the sine policy on 100 fresh seeds per setting. At the minimum
budget it succeeds on 92/100 easy, 91/100 medium, and 98/100 hard tasks.
All planned tasks remain in the denominator, including ten with zero forcing
and zero response, for which the operator coefficients cannot be recovered.
At full budget the counts are 92/100, 85/100, and 93/100: adding rows can
worsen coefficient recovery in the finite-precision system. These are
post-design validation results for the numerical policy, not fresh model runs.

\input{paper_artifacts/sine_generalization_table.tex}

Two mathematical checks help explain these results. First, distinct operators
can sometimes generate exactly the same response and source. We construct an
admissible polynomial family with this property; it is an example, not a
classification of the 30 archived tasks. Second, small singular values can
amplify the forward solver's and quadrature's consistency errors. A small
residual then need not imply an accurate coefficient vector. The observation
model, proof, reconstruction checks, and full controls appear in
Appendices~\ref{app:math}--\ref{app:controls}.

These controls establish the appropriate interpretation of the model study.
The environment exposes choices in an investigation and their consequences,
while the current task set does not demonstrate an advantage from adaptive
language-model measurement selection. Whether adaptation helps on fresh tasks
under matched information budgets remains an experimental question.

\section{Related Work}

Interactive scientific-agent environments such as DiscoveryWorld, BoxingGym,
and SciGym study sequential observation and experimentation
\citep{jansen2024discoveryworld,gandhi2025boxinggym,duan2025scigym}.
Our setting contributes a small inverse problem in which measurement matrices,
coefficient truth, and numerical sensitivity can be examined directly.
The cognitive framework of \citet{burnell2026cognitive} motivates the original
behavioral questions, while calibration analysis distinguishes stated
confidence from empirical correctness \citep{guo2017calibration}.

LLM-SR and LLM-SRBench address scientific equation discovery
\citep{shojaee2025llmsr,shojaee2025llmsrbench}; here the operator family is fixed
and a numerical tool performs coefficient fitting. Weak SINDy also uses
integral formulations for identification \citep{messenger2021wsindy}.
Experimental design provides a reference for selecting informative observations
\citep{mackay1992information,alexanderian2021oed}. Identifiability results depend
on the observation operator \citep{egger2014simultaneous,browning2024structural,browning2025parabolic};
we therefore analyze the particular fixed-solution observation model used here.

\section{Discussion and Conclusion}

The benchmark records an investigation as well as an answer. Tool calls show
how models allocate activity; paired error observations connect revision to
endpoint accuracy; confidence reports can be checked against the estimates
available at the time. These records allow distinct questions to be asked of
runs with equal success counts, without treating the resulting metrics as
interchangeable measures of cognition.

The numerical controls delimit that interpretation. A fixed measurement design
already solves the reconstructed archived tasks, and fresh tasks expose both
unidentifiable cases and numerical failures. The present evidence covers 30
shared archived tasks, bundled difficulty settings, and one retained trajectory
per model--task pair. Incomplete attempt histories and source provenance limit
claims about provider reliability and exact historical reconstruction.

A prospective study could compare fixed, randomized, and adaptive policies under
one budget accounting for every truth-dependent observation. Retaining all
attempts, exact prompts, model settings, and internal numerical matrices would
support closer replay. Requesting confidence at common stages would make
monitoring comparisons more interpretable. Additional source or boundary
conditions could test whether agents choose observations that resolve ambiguity.
The current environment and records provide a concrete starting point for these
experiments: scientific reasoning studied through observable measurement choices,
feedback, and revision.

This work develops a controlled environment for studying scientific-agent

\section{Mathematical Model and Identifiability}
\label{app:math}
\subsection{Observation Matrices and Coefficient Recovery}

Equations~\eqref{eq:pde}--\eqref{eq:weak} define the forward problem and the
observed weak identity. The coefficient basis uses ordinary shifted Legendre
polynomials on \([0,1]\), for example \(P_0(x)=1\) and \(P_1(x)=2x-1\).
They are orthogonal but not orthonormal. At the continuum level, take smooth
coefficients with positive diffusion, an observed solution \(u\in H_0^1(0,1)\),
and tests \(\varphi_k\in H_0^1(0,1)\). The zero endpoint values remove the
boundary term in integration by parts, so the weak measurements require only
first derivatives of \(u\).

Each test supplies one operator constraint. Define
\begin{align}
 D_{kj}&=\int P_j u'\varphi_k'\dd x,& B_{kj}&=\int P_j u'\varphi_k\dd x,\nonumber\\
 C_{kj}&=\int P_j u\varphi_k\dd x,& F_{kj}&=\int P_j\varphi_k\dd x,
 \label{eq:matrices}
\end{align}
with integrals over \((0,1)\). After \(K\) queries, let
\(A=[D\mid B\mid C]\in\R^{K\times3N}\),
\(\theta=(\theta_a^\top,\theta_b^\top,\theta_c^\top)^\top\), and
\(r_k=\int f\varphi_k\dd x\). The tool separately fits
\begin{equation}
 \widehat\theta\in\arg\min_\theta\|A\theta-r\|_2,\qquad
 \widehat\theta_f\in\arg\min_{\theta_f}\|F\theta_f-r\|_2.
 \label{eq:fits}
\end{equation}
For a nonunique fit, it selects the minimum Euclidean norm solution under its
numerical singular-value cutoff. Both fits use the measured vector \(r\), so
operator recovery does not require a preceding source estimate. Source moments
provide information in addition to the observed solution.

The tool constructs each matrix row internally. The agent receives the source
moment after a query, then coefficient estimates and selected diagnostics when
it requests a solve. The full joint matrices and spectra in the analysis are
reconstructed from archived expressions. The forward solution is computed by
finite differences, rather than expanded in the chosen test functions. The
protocol requires \(K\ge4N\) before a solve, although the operator fit has
\(3N\) unknowns; this rule does not guarantee rank or uniqueness.

\subsection{Reference Prompt and Numerical Implementation}
\label{app:prompt}

The reference implementation supplies the equation, weak identity, and parameter
count, but does not name the Legendre basis. Table~\ref{tab:tools_main} lists the
available operations. A response may contain several free commands and at most
one stored query; submission cannot share a response with a query. The reference
rules explicitly encourage using the free tools:
\begin{quote}\small
\texttt{COMPUTE: solve} is free and unlimited---call it after every few queries
to track how your estimates evolve. This is good experimental practice.
\texttt{COMPUTE: check} is free---use it to verify your estimates against new
test functions.
\end{quote}
The enhanced initial message asks the model to explain its choices and adds:
\begin{quote}\small
Note: in elliptic inverse problems of this type, the three coefficient functions
\(a(x),b(x),c(x)\) are not equally identifiable from weak-form data. The
difficulty of recovering each coefficient depends on the magnitude of its
contribution to the weak-form equations relative to the others---and this varies
with the solution \(u(x)\) and the test functions you choose.
\end{quote}
The confidence instruction is:
\begin{quote}\small
After each \texttt{COMPUTE: solve}, report your confidence (0--100\%) that each
coefficient vector \((a,b,c,f)\) is within 0.1 of the true value.
\end{quote}
It prescribes a reporting format and one sentence about uncertainty, but no
coordinate norm. Our correctness event \(E_q<0.1\) is the analysis's explicit
operationalization. These excerpts describe the pinned reference implementation;
the logs do not establish the exact full system prompt and source revision for
every historical provider call. The accompanying
\path{reference_prompt_components.txt} supplies the base prompts for all three
settings, protocol, confidence instruction, and enhanced initial message,
extracted from the available source. Optional runtime overrides and turn
reminders are identified as outside that reference extract.

The expression parser accepts polynomial, trigonometric, exponential, and
localized forms subject to sampled-value and endpoint checks. Numerical
acceptance does not certify membership in the continuum test space; predefined
controls use smooth functions. There is no common amplitude normalization.
Free consistency checks expose source moments without adding rows to the fitted
system, while solution evaluations and term-integral diagnostics are also free.

\end{table}

The generator checks diffusion positivity on 500 spatial points;
\(a_{\min}\) controls the constant diffusion coefficient, not a certified
pointwise lower bound. Reaction can have either sign, so diffusion positivity
alone does not guarantee a unique continuum forward solution. The evaluation
takes the computed solution as data. Dimension, scale, sparsity, and resolution
vary together, so the difficulty labels describe bundled settings.

The reference implementation uses second-order finite differences for the
solution, a fourth-order interior derivative with boundary stencils, and
composite Simpson quadrature on the solution grid. Each stored matrix entry and
right-hand-side value is rounded to 12 decimal places; the fit is unweighted
least squares. The model sees coefficients rounded to eight decimals, while
final scoring uses the internally retained unrounded estimate. Symbolic test
functions are differentiated with a fourth-order centered stencil and step
\(10^{-6}\), independently of solution-grid spacing. These choices affect
recovery when singular values are small.

\subsection{Coefficient Error, Uniqueness, and Stability}
\label{sec:identifiability}

Equation~\eqref{eq:error} defines the coefficient-coordinate error. For each
field, \(\|\widehat q-q\|_{L^\infty(0,1)}\le NE_q\), since
\(|P_j|\le1\). Thus a small coefficient error controls field error, but the
same numerical threshold for these two quantities is not equivalent.

Three questions should be separated. \emph{Structural identifiability} asks
whether different coefficient triples can produce the same \(u,f\), even with
all admissible weak measurements. \emph{Measurement selection} asks whether a
finite set of tests distinguishes triples in the chosen coefficient space.
\emph{Numerical stability} asks how small data and discretization errors affect
the recovered coefficients. Full column rank of an exact moment matrix formed
from the continuum solution would certify uniqueness in the operator coefficient space.
Full rank after numerical approximation and rounding need not do so.
Separate full-rank blocks \(D,B,C\) do not exclude dependencies between blocks.

To see the role of numerical error, write \(A_h,r_h\) for the assembled system
and \(\theta_*\) for the true operator coefficients. Its consistency defect is
\(e_h=A_h\theta_*-r_h\). The forward solve, derivative approximation,
quadrature, and rounding need not preserve the weak identity exactly, so this
defect can be nonzero even at the truth. If \(A_h\) has full column rank,
exact least squares for that assembled system gives
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \widehat\theta-\theta_*&=-A_h^\dagger e_h,\\
 \|\widehat\theta-\theta_*\|_2&\le\frac{\|e_h\|_2}{\sigma_{\min}(A_h)}.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:stability}
\end{equation}
Here \(A_h^\dagger\) is the pseudoinverse. The bound concerns the operator
fit; the score also includes the separately fitted source. A small fitted weak
residual \(\|A_h\widehat\theta-r_h\|_2\) need not imply a small coefficient error: a small singular value can amplify a small
consistency defect. The forward finite-difference residual, fitted weak residual,
and coefficient score therefore answer different questions.

Let \(\Poly_d\) denote polynomials of degree at most \(d\). The following
example shows an ambiguity that persists even before discretization.

\begin{proposition}[An admissible nonidentifiable family]
\label{prop:nullity}
Let \(u(x)=x(1-x)\), \(N\ge2\), and
\(T(\delta a,\delta b,\delta c)=-(\delta a u')'+\delta b u'+\delta c u\)
with domain \(\Poly_{N-1}^3\). Then \(T\) has rank \(N+2\) and nullity
\(2N-2\). Every element of its kernel leaves all continuum weak-form
measurements unchanged at fixed \(u,f\).
\end{proposition}

Even arbitrarily many exact weak measurements cannot distinguish operators
separated by these kernel directions. Appendix~\ref{app:proof} gives the proof
and examples with positive diffusion and nonnegative reaction. This quadratic-
solution family is an admissible counterexample, not a classification of the
30 sampled instances. There is also a positive-probability
zero-excitation event in the generator: each forcing coefficient is zeroed
independently with probability \(s\), giving
\(\Pr(f\equiv0)=s^N\), or 0.09, 0.008, and 0.0001 in the three settings.
When \(f\equiv0\), under homogeneous boundary data the native forward solve returns \(u=0\) when
its matrix is nonsingular. Then every operator design row vanishes, irrespective
of the test function, so no measurement policy can identify \(a,b,c\) from
these observations. The nonzero-source construction in
Proposition~\ref{prop:nullity} is a separate ambiguity mechanism.

\subsection{Structural Nonuniqueness: Proof and Examples}
\label{app:proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:nullity}]
Let \(\Poly_d\) denote polynomials of degree at most \(d\).
For \(u=x(1-x)\), all three terms in \(T\) lie in \(\Poly_{N+1}\),
so the rank is at most \(N+2\). The reaction component contains
\(u\Poly_{N-1}\). Taking constant \(\delta a\) gives a nonzero constant
because \(-u''=2\), and taking constant \(\delta b\) gives \(u'=1-2x\).
These span \(\Poly_1\). Polynomial division by the quadratic \(u\) gives
\(\Poly_{N+1}=u\Poly_{N-1}\oplus\Poly_1\), proving surjectivity and rank
\(N+2\). The domain has dimension \(3N\), so rank--nullity gives
\(\dim\ker T=2N-2\). Multiplication by any admissible test function and
integration by parts shows that a kernel element changes no weak measurement.
\end{proof}

For \(N=2\), \(u=x(1-x)\) and \(f=3\), the operators
\[
 a=\tfrac32+\varepsilon,\qquad b=\varepsilon(4x-2),\qquad c=-8\varepsilon
\]
all satisfy the same equation, since
\(2\varepsilon+\varepsilon(4x-2)(1-2x)-8\varepsilon x(1-x)=0\).
For example \(\varepsilon=-1/8\) preserves positive diffusion and gives
nonnegative reaction. For \(N\ge3\), the base choice
\(a=1,b=0,c=1,f=2+u\) belongs to the class. Sufficiently small perturbations
in every kernel direction preserve positive diffusion and reaction, giving a relatively open \((2N-2)\)-dimensional family in the affine kernel
of admissible coefficient tuples with identical observations.
Neither construction is asserted to occur among the 30 sampled tasks.
The audit script also verifies ranks over exact rational arithmetic for
\(N=2,\ldots,8\); these checks supplement the proof rather than replace it.

\section{Numerical Controls and Reconstruction}
\label{app:controls}
\subsection{Numerical Controls and Replay}
\label{sec:controls}

For the controls, we regenerate all 30 tasks from their recorded seeds, verify
exact equality with saved ground-truth coefficient vectors, and evaluate
non-language-model query policies under the same accepted-query limits and
supplied query/solve interface. The policies use no hidden coefficients, solution values, measurement responses,
or diagnostics to select expressions. Fixed sine uses the Dirichlet Laplacian eigenfunctions
\(\varphi_k=\sin(k\pi x)\), \(k=1,\ldots,K_{\max}\), where
\(K_{\max}=6N\) is the
stored-query budget. Boundary Legendre multiplies a polynomial test by the
endpoint-vanishing factor \(x(1-x)\), giving \(x(1-x)P_{k-1}(x)\).
The implementation uses factored expressions for numerical stability. Random mixed draws uniformly
from five admissible families, using ten independently seeded query lists per
setting, reused across the ten PDE seeds. Its fixed parameter ranges and exact
expressions are released in the experiment output. All controls solve at every
query count from \(4N\) through \(6N\); additional solves do not alter their
predefined query lists. These controls test whether simple nonadaptive policies suffice on these
instances; they do not reproduce the models' free-action
or stopping decisions.

For the numerical audit, we replay the archived query expressions, compare the
returned source moments with the logs, and reconstruct joint matrix spectra.
We report both raw and unit-column-norm spectra, with explicit relative singular
value cutoffs rather than a binary rank label alone. We additionally multiply
each reconstructed row and its source moment by a common amplitude before
12-decimal rounding, leaving the query sets fixed and refitting all stored rows. This audit scores
8-decimal diagnostic estimates, unlike unrounded primary final scores. In exact arithmetic this common scaling leaves least squares unchanged;
its effect here tests the absolute quantization rule. It is a post hoc numerical
sensitivity analysis, rather than an optimized query policy or a model rerun.

We also refine the forward and quadrature grids together by factors two and
four. Original-resolution primary controls use the native dense solver.
Refinements use an independently assembled banded solver for the same finite-
difference equations; we validate its solution and score sensitivity against
native solves on all 30 tasks. Refinement changes the numerical environment,
rather than adding independent tasks or measuring observation-noise robustness.

To test specificity to the archived seeds, we additionally evaluate the fixed
sine policy on 100 fresh seeds (1001--1100) in each difficulty, with the original
generator and grid settings. Every planned task is retained, including zero
forcing and numerical failures; there are no retries or filters. This is
validation after design selection, not a preregistered comparison or a new
language-model experiment. We report outcomes on all 100 tasks per setting,
with Wilson intervals and descriptive zero/nonzero-source breakdowns. Reused
seed labels across difficulties are not pooled into independent sampling units.

\subsection{Simple Nonadaptive Queries Suffice on These Tasks}
\label{sec:baseline_results}

The fixed sine policy reaches \(E<0.01\) on all ten tasks in each difficulty,
already at the minimum \(4N\) stored queries (8, 12, and 16). It retains
30/30 success at the full \(6N\) budget. Boundary Legendre succeeds on 29/30
at full budget. Random mixed succeeds on 100/100 easy, 92/100 medium, and
85/100 hard policy--task combinations (Table~\ref{tab:controls}); these reuse
ten PDE instances per setting and are not 100 independent tasks.
Across random query-list replicates, hard success ranges from 6/10 to 10/10.
Figure~\ref{fig:controls} shows recovery at every permitted solve count.

matrices were not retained, we compare newly reconstructed controls with

Grid refinement improves numerical errors: for fixed sine at full budget,
median \(E\) changes from \(9.47\times10^{-5}\) to

boundary Legendre reaches 10/10 medium successes. The original-grid native and
banded solves agree on both success thresholds at every checkpoint across all
360 paired policy--task runs. Thus the banded implementation does not change
these threshold comparisons, although individual numerical errors differ.

\subsection{Fresh Seeds Expose the Limits of the Archive Result}

Table~\ref{tab:fresh} validates the fixed sine policy on all 300 fresh tasks.
At the minimum query budget, success is 92/100 easy, 91/100 medium, and
98/100 hard. At full budget, it is 92/100, 85/100, and 93/100, respectively.
All 300 executions complete without errors; no task is retried or excluded.
Seven easy and three medium tasks have zero forcing, exactly zero solutions,
and joint operator rank zero at both budgets. All ten fail, as predicted by the
zero-excitation argument. Excluding them only for a descriptive breakdown,
full-budget recovery is 92/93 easy, 85/97 medium, and 93/100 hard.



Thus the archived 30/30 result does not establish perfect generator-wide
recovery. Nonzero-source failures also remain, and increasing the stored-query
budget can reduce the number of coefficient-threshold successes in the same
fixed policy. More rows do not guarantee lower coefficient error in an
inconsistent finite-precision system. This validation evaluates one selected
numerical policy; it does not determine whether adaptive policies improve
recovery on fresh tasks. It does establish why a benchmark needs both simple
controls and a stated treatment of unidentifiable instances.

\subsection{What Numerical Reconstruction Can Establish}
\label{sec:numerics}

All 30 ground-truth coefficient vectors regenerate exactly, and the reconstructed reference
implementation reproduces all 2,874 archived query source moments exactly at
the stored precision. Source-moment agreement does not test the historical forward solution or
unretained operator matrix: the source moment depends only on \(f\), the test
function, and quadrature. It does not establish exact historical solve replay: across 687 solves, the median maximum deviation from the logged
8-decimal coefficient vectors is \(2.55\times10^{-5}\), and the largest is
3.50. The archive lacks historical internal matrices and sufficient environment
provenance to localize every discrepancy. New controls and spectral diagnostics
must therefore be identified as reconstructions.

All 180 reconstructed final stored-query matrices have full column rank at
NumPy's default floating-point tolerance. After unit-column scaling, however,
the median \(\sigma_{\min}/\sigma_{\max}\) is
\(2.26\times10^{-7}\), \(5.53\times10^{-10}\), and
\(1.80\times10^{-10}\) for easy, medium, and hard runs. At a declared
relative cutoff of \(10^{-8}\), 13/60, 42/60, and 37/60 matrices are rank
deficient. These are diagnostics of limited numerical resolution, not continuum
nonuniqueness certificates for the sampled instances.

a success event even when the underlying span of measurements is unchanged.
They do not establish that larger amplitude universally improves recovery.

\subsection{Task-Level Comparisons and Confidence}

The comparison unit is the shared PDE seed within each difficulty, not an
individual coefficient, solve, or confidence report. Each model has ten tasks
per setting. We report Wilson 95\% intervals for marginal success proportions
and paired task resampling for success differences (20,000 draws; seed 20261020). Exploratory pairwise tests
condition on discordant tasks, with Holm correction across the 15 model pairs
within each difficulty. There are no repeated model draws from which to estimate
within-task response variance. Empirical paired-bootstrap intervals can
degenerate when all ten observed task differences agree. These intervals describe uncertainty under task
resampling assumptions and do not resolve selection into the scored archive.

A confidence report states the model's probability that a coefficient block
is accurate. We pair it only with a solve returned on a strictly earlier turn.
Its correctness event is \(E_q<0.1\), separately for
\(q\in\{a,b,c\}\), which differs from final-run success \(E<0.01\).
All reports and coefficient blocks within a run are
kept together when resampling; run-balanced scores avoid giving a model more
weight merely because it reports more often. Action counts, first-to-last solve
improvement, and expression-family entropy are descriptive diagnostics. They do
not independently validate planning, learning, or information gain.

\section{Behavioral Figures and Their Interpretation}
\label{app:behavior}

The main figures summarize the same six models across easy, medium, and hard
tasks. Compact plot labels abbreviate Gemini as G, Claude Opus as Opus, and
Claude Sonnet as Sonnet; GPT mini denotes GPT-5.4 mini. Stacked tool-use bars use means, so their heights are
mean combined counts of these three action types. Improvement and entropy bars show means alongside
median ticks and individual observations.

All 159 repeated-solve ratios are available; the 21 remaining runs contain a
single solve and have no plotted ratio. The previous summary-file convention
assigned one to these cases, but those values are not reused as observed
revisions. Means and medians condition on the eligible repeated-solve runs,
whose counts appear below the model names. A large reduction can reflect a
poor initial estimate or the ordinary benefit of additional measurements.

Query-family entropy is
\(H=-\sum_{i=1}^5p_i\log_2p_i/\log_2 5\), with \(0\log0=0\).
The five categories are polynomial, trigonometric, exponential, localized, and
other, assigned by a heuristic string classifier. The archived taxonomy is
recorded at the final solve; later queries in six runs are absent from it.
A high entropy does not imply linearly independent or useful measurements.

The confidence plots use disjoint bins \([l,h)\), with 1 included in the last
bin, and show mean confidence against empirical correctness. Reports are
matched only to strictly earlier solves, since a solve requested in a report's
own response has not yet been returned. The archive contains 733 confidence reports: 707 have a preceding solve and
26 are excluded. Causal matching changes the solve paired to 71 reports relative
to the legacy query-count rule; 827 observations lie on interior bin boundaries
that the old plots could count twice. The retained reports contribute
2,121 dependent observations for \(a,b,c\), excluding \(f\). Individual
coefficient reports are not treated as independent tasks.

For run \(i\) with \(m_i\) retained coefficient observations, the run-balanced
Brier score is
\[
 \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n\frac{1}{m_i}\sum_{r=1}^{m_i}(p_{ir}-y_{ir})^2.
\]
We also report the signed gap obtained by replacing the square with
\(p_{ir}-y_{ir}\); positive values indicate average overconfidence.
This gap is calibration in the large, not expected calibration error or a full
calibration criterion. Intervals resample whole supported runs with 20,000 draws.
There are 179 supported runs: Gemini Flash hard has nine and all other cells ten.
Run balancing does not align reporting stages, query counts, or base accuracies.
Thus these scores cannot compare intrinsic metacognitive ability without a
matched reporting schedule and correctness distribution.
